Before you read

A world beside our own

In this parallel Oxford, every person has a dæmon: an animal companion that expresses part of the person’s inner life. Lyra is a bold, inquisitive girl raised at Jordan College. As children disappear and powerful adults hide what they know, her curiosity draws her into a much larger mystery.

The chapter titles and numbering below follow the common English-language edition. Some editions use the title Northern Lights instead of The Golden Compass.

The story so far

Chapter-by-chapter guide

Chapter 1

The Decanter of Tokay

Lyra sneaks into the Scholars’ Retiring Room and hides in a wardrobe. From there she witnesses Lord Asriel’s return from the North. He presents evidence of mysterious Dust and an image of the aurora that seems to show another world. Lyra also notices an attempt to poison Asriel’s wine and warns him in time. His account fascinates the Scholars, but their decisions reveal that knowledge can be treated as a threat.

Key idea: Curiosity can uncover truths adults would rather keep hidden.

Chapter 2

The Idea of North

Lyra thinks about the North and Lord Asriel’s expedition while the Scholars debate his findings. The chapter gives more context for Dust, the northern lights, and the fear surrounding Asriel’s research. Lyra’s admiration for him grows, even though she has only fragments of the story and does not yet understand the danger around him.

Key idea: A mystery can inspire wonder and fear at the same time.

Chapter 3

Lyra’s Jordan

Lyra’s life at the college is shown through her friendship with Roger, the kitchen boy, and her adventures with Pantalaimon. News spreads that children are being taken by people called the Gobblers. When the elegant Mrs. Coulter arrives, she offers Lyra a chance to leave Jordan and travel with her as an assistant. Lyra accepts, excited by the promise of a new life.

Key idea: A tempting opportunity may conceal risks that are hard to see.

Chapter 4

The Alethiometer

Before Lyra leaves, the Master gives her a rare instrument called an alethiometer and tells her to keep it secret, especially from Mrs. Coulter. It resembles a compass but answers questions through symbols. Lyra does not yet know how to read it, yet it becomes a quiet sign that the adults around her know more about her future than they are saying.

Key idea: Truth is valuable, but learning to interpret it takes care.

Chapter 5

The Cocktail Party

In London, Mrs. Coulter introduces Lyra to her polished social world. At a party, Lyra overhears guests discussing the General Oblation Board and the missing children. She begins to understand that Mrs. Coulter is connected to the Gobblers. When Mrs. Coulter’s golden monkey searches for the alethiometer, Lyra realizes she is in danger and decides to escape.

Key idea: A charming public image can hide a troubling purpose.

Chapter 6

The Throwing Nets

Alone in London, Lyra tries to find her way through unfamiliar streets. Two men attempt to capture her with a net, but Tony Costa and other Gyptians intervene and rescue her. Lyra is taken aboard their boat and learns that the Gyptians are organizing to search for the children who have been kidnapped. She hopes their journey can help her find Roger.

Key idea: Trust and cooperation can turn a frightening moment into a chance for help.

Ideas for a project

Possible themes

Curiosity and discovery

Lyra’s habit of asking questions opens the story’s mysteries, from Dust to the alethiometer.

Truth and secrecy

Adults conceal information, while Lyra pieces together clues and begins to test what she hears.

Appearance and reality

Mrs. Coulter’s charm contrasts with the disturbing clues Lyra finds about her work.

Power and control

The Scholars, the Oblation Board, and the people who capture children all hold power over others.

Friendship and loyalty

Lyra’s bond with Roger shapes her choices, while the Gyptians’ rescue shows solidarity.

Trust and judgment

Lyra must decide whom to trust, even when she has incomplete evidence.

Freedom and safety

Leaving Jordan promises independence, but Lyra discovers that freedom can bring danger.

Growing up

Lyra moves from protected college life into situations where she must judge adults and act for herself.

Knowledge and responsibility

Asriel’s research and the alethiometer raise questions about who should control knowledge and how it should be used.

Belonging and community

Jordan College, Mrs. Coulter’s London circle, and the Gyptians offer very different forms of belonging.

Identity and dæmons

The close relationship between people and dæmons makes inner life visible in this world.

Fear and courage

Lyra feels fear but still warns Asriel, escapes Mrs. Coulter, and looks for a way to help Roger.

People to know

Characters in Chapters 1–6

Lyra Belacqua

Adventurous and quick-thinking, Lyra begins to question the adults who guide her.

Pantalaimon

Lyra’s dæmon and closest companion, able to change animal form while Lyra is still young.

Lord Asriel

Lyra’s formidable uncle, whose northern research concerns Dust and the aurora.

Mrs. Marisa Coulter

A sophisticated traveler who takes Lyra to London; clues soon make Lyra question her motives.

Roger Parslow

Lyra’s close friend at Jordan College, whose disappearance gives her a personal reason to act.

Tony Costa and the Gyptians

Canal people who rescue Lyra and are preparing to search for the missing children.

For class discussion

Questions to explore

  1. Why does Lyra hide in the Retiring Room, and what does that reveal about her?
  2. How do the Scholars respond differently to Lord Asriel’s discoveries?
  3. What details make Mrs. Coulter seem trustworthy at first? Which details change Lyra’s view?
  4. Why might the alethiometer be given to Lyra in secret?
  5. How does Lyra’s connection to Roger influence her decisions?
  6. Which theme from these chapters would make the strongest presentation? Use one scene as evidence.
